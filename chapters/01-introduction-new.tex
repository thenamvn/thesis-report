\section{Background of Gamified E-Learning and Commercial Engagement}
\label{sec:intro_background}
The landscape of global engagement—whether in academic institutions or commercial enterprises—is undergoing a seismic transformation, significantly accelerated by the relentless pace of technological innovation. The traditional paradigm of passive instruction and static advertising is increasingly being augmented, and in many cases replaced, by interactive, experience-driven digital environments. This has propelled gamified platforms from niche alternatives to mainstream pillars of engagement across educational, corporate, and retail sectors. However, the rapid migration to online platforms has also cast a harsh light on the limitations of first-generation systems. Many of these platforms were designated as little more than digital repositories for content, leading to passive, uninspiring experiences that result in high dropout rates and a demonstrable lack of user retention.

In response to these deficiencies, two powerful technological and pedagogical trends have emerged as catalysts for the next generation of digital engagement: \textbf{gamification} and \textbf{artificial intelligence (AI)}. Gamification, the strategic application of game-design elements and principles in non-game contexts, has been empirically shown to enhance motivation, knowledge retention, and overall engagement by tapping into intrinsic human desires for achievement, competition, and social connection \citep{oliveira2023}. By embedding elements such as points, leaderboards, mission-based objectives, and tangible rewards, hosts can transform learning or brand interaction from a passive chore into an active, enjoyable pursuit.

Concurrently, breakthroughs in artificial intelligence, particularly in the domain of generative models and natural language processing (NLP), are unlocking unprecedented opportunities to automate, personalize, and enrich content. AI can now function as a powerful assistant for educators and business owners alike, capable of generating diverse, high-quality assessment questions, adapting content difficulty, and providing instantaneous feedback. This technological leverage alleviates the significant administrative and content-creation burden while paving the way for a more tailored and efficient journey for participants \citep{kasneci2023}.

This thesis is situated at the confluence of these transformative trends, addressing a dual need in both \textbf{Education (EdTech)} and \textbf{Online-to-Offline (O2O) Commerce}.
In an educational context, it addresses the need for active learning. In a commercial context, it addresses the "O2O Gap"—the difficulty businesses face in converting digital social media engagement into physical foot traffic. The TEKUTOKO project was conceived to address these needs by designing a platform that synergistically integrates mission-based gamification with powerful AI-driven content generation, all underpinned by a robust, scalable, and modern software architecture.

Furthermore, the challenges of ensuring integrity—whether academic honesty in exams or voucher validity in marketing campaigns—have become increasingly critical. The development of effective yet non-invasive monitoring solutions represents a significant challenge that this research addresses trough innovative browser-based techniques \citep{balderas2021}.

The convergence of these capabilities—gamification for engagement, AI for content generation, O2O voucher systems for physical conversion, and automated integrity monitoring—presents a unique opportunity to reimagine the digital engagement experience. Modern cloud-based architectures, particularly microservice-based designs, provide the scalable infrastructure necessary to support these sophisticated features while maintaining system reliability and performance \citep{richards2020}.

\section{Motivations}
\label{sec:intro_motivations}

Despite the rapid proliferation of digital platforms, their effectiveness is frequently undermined by a set of persistent and interconnected challenges. This research identifies and directly addresses five primary problems that hinder the potential of both digital education and gamified marketing:

\subsection{Low User Engagement in Digital Environments}
Many platforms fail to capture and sustain user interest, leading to passive consumption of information rather than active, cognitive participation. In education, this manifests as poor learning outcomes and attrition; in commerce, it appears as "banner blindness" where users ignore static advertising.

The absence of interactive, motivating, and socially engaging elements makes the digital experience feel isolating. Research has consistently demonstrated that active engagement is a critical predictor of success in online environments \citep{fredricks2019}. Traditional platforms often replicate passive formats (lecture videos or image ads) without leveraging interactive potential. This passive approach fails to activate the psychological needs for autonomy, competence, and relatedness that are fundamental to intrinsic motivation according to Self-Determination Theory \citep{deci2020}.

Gamification presents a promising solution by incorporating game-design elements. However, the key is not simply adding superficial badges, but thoughtfully integrating them to support goals—whether mastering a subject or understanding a brand product—while satisfying the user's psychological needs \citep{oliveira2023}.

\subsection{The Online-to-Offline (O2O) Conversion Gap}
A critical challenge, particularly for commercial hosts (e.g., local businesses), is the disconnect between digital engagement and physical action. While social media interactions are easily generated, converting a "like" or an "answer" into a physical store visit is difficult.

Small business owners lack accessible tools to create campaigns that effectively bridge this gap. Traditional loyalty strategies are often bifurcated: digital ads generate awareness but not foot traffic, while paper coupons are difficult to track and distribute. There is a distinct lack of "Play-to-Earn" models that allow hosts to verify physical visits and securely reward users for their demonstrated knowledge or engagement.

\subsection{High Content Creation Overhead for Hosts}
The process of creating high-quality, diverse, and stimulating content—especially varied quizzes, assessments, and interactive activities—is an exceptionally time-consuming and labor-intensive task. This applies equally to educators creating exams and business owners creating promotional trivia.

Hosts report spending substantial time developing questions, creating distractors, and ensuring accuracy. This significant overhead often leads to a reliance on static, repetitive materials that quickly become outdated. The administrative burden takes time away from other high-value activities, such as personalized mentoring or customer relationship management.

Recent advances in large language models (LLMs) and generative AI offer transformative potential. AI systems can now generate contextually appropriate assessment questions at scale, significantly reducing the "time-to-publish" \citep{kasneci2023}. However, successful integration requires maintaining human oversight to ensure quality and brand/pedagogical safety.

\subsection{Cheating and Fraud Risks in Online Environments}
The remote nature of online interaction presents profound challenges to maintaining fairness. In education, this manifests as academic dishonesty (cheating on exams). In a commercial context, it manifests as fraud—where users might exploit system loopholes to claim vouchers without genuine engagement.

Traditional solutions are often extreme. High-stakes proctoring (webcams) creates privacy concerns and anxiety \citep{balderas2021}, while loose systems lack accountability. There is a critical need for a middle-ground approach: a lightweight, browser-based monitoring system that detects suspicious behavior (e.g., tab switching, pasting answers) to ensure fairness for students and validity for commercial hosts, without invasive surveillance.

\subsection{Architectural Rigidity of Monolithic Systems}
Many legacy systems are built using monolithic architectures, where all functional components are tightly coupled. As a platform grows to support diverse needs—handling real-time game logic, AI processing, and secure voucher transactions—this rigidity becomes a bottleneck.

Monolithic architectures make it difficult to scale specific components independently. For instance, a surge in AI question generation traffic should not degrade the performance of the real-time gameplay service. A microservice architecture is necessary to enable independent scaling, technology diversity, and improved fault isolation \citep{richards2020}.

This thesis posits that these four problems are not independent but are deeply intertwined. The TEKUTOKO platform is therefore designed as a holistic solution to address these interconnected challenges simultaneously, providing an integrated approach that leverages modern technologies and architectural patterns to create a more effective, engaging, and secure e-learning environment.

\section{Research Objectives}
\label{sec:intro_objectives}

The primary objective of this research is to design, implement, and rigorously evaluate the TEKUTOKO platform, a multifaceted ecosystem bridging e-learning and O2O commercial engagement through deep gamification, AI-driven content generation, and scalable microservices.

To achieve this overarching goal, the following specific objectives are defined:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{To design and develop a dual-purpose web platform integrating deep gamification} for both learners and commercial hosts. Features include mission-based rooms, competitive leaderboards, and \textbf{Location-Based Service (LBS)} functionality that connects digital interactions with physical locations (e.g., finding a "Knowledge Quest" at a local cafe).
    
    \item \textbf{To implement an O2O (Online-to-Offline) Voucher System} that serves as a tangible reward mechanism. This allows hosts to issue digital vouchers based on quiz performance, which users can redeem at specific physical locations via secure QR code verification, thereby driving measurable foot traffic and validating the "Play-to-Earn" model.
    
    \item \textbf{To implement and evaluate an AI-powered content module} that automatically generates diverse, high-quality quiz questions from provided topics (for educators) or product descriptions (for commercial hosts). This module aims to significantly reduce content creation workload while ensuring pedagogical and contextual relevance through human oversight.
    
    \item \textbf{To integrate a non-invasive, browser-based anti-cheating system} capable of monitoring suspicious activities (e.g., tab-switching, inactivity). This serves a dual role: preserving academic integrity in exams and protecting the validity of commercial reward campaigns, respecting user privacy while maintaining fairness.
    
    \item \textbf{To architect the entire system using a microservice-based approach} to ensure high levels of scalability, maintainability, and resilience. The architecture should enable independent deployment of distinct components (e.g., Game Service vs. Voucher Service), support technology diversity, and maintain availability under varying load conditions.
    
    \item \textbf{To empirically evaluate the platform's effectiveness} through a mixed-methods approach, measuring its impact on user engagement (SUS scores), operational efficiency (time savings for hosts), system performance (load testing), and the integrity of its verification mechanisms.
\end{enumerate}

These objectives collectively address the research gaps identified in the literature and provide a comprehensive framework for developing next-generation e-learning platforms that are more engaging, efficient, secure, and scalable than current solutions.
